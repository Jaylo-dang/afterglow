## 2. My findings

### Finding A
- Where: https://afterglow-sage.vercel.app/ , the filter box at the top of the "Getting to
  the Spots" section, whose placeholder reads "Filter spots by name, bus stop, or road...".

- What I did, what I saw: Laptop Firefox.
  First, in Sunset/Sunrise mode set to Sunrise and with location already granted, I typed
  "Devonshire" into the filter box (a road near Somerset in Singapore). The list emptied
  and one line of grey text appeared:
      No shooting spots found matching "Devonshire" facing east for sunrise.
  Second, in Sunset mode and with location not granted, I typed "somerset" and got the
  same shape of empty result:
      No shooting spots found matching "somerset" facing west for sunset.
  The behaviour was the same in both modes and in both location states, and it reproduced
  in laptop Safari as well.
  The words "or road" in the placeholder led me to believe I could type any road name. In
  fact the box only searches within the names, bus stop names and road names carried by
  the five shooting spots themselves (Marina Gdns Dr / Telok Blangah Cres / Beach View /
  East Coast Pk Svc Rd / New Punggol Rd).

- Which heuristic: 5, Error Prevention

- Screen or system: Screen. What this finding asks to be repaired is two pieces of
  wording: the placeholder and the empty-result message. The page already knows that the
  box only searches within the five spots, so saying that plainly needs no new data.

- Severity, and why: 3, driven by how often it happens. Everyone who opens this section
  sees the words "or road" in the placeholder, and any of them may type a road name
  because of it.

- The repair: The placeholder no longer promises something the box cannot do, and when
  the result is empty the screen states that the box only searches within the five
  shooting spots and points to another way of finding the spot the visitor wants.

### Finding B
- Where: https://afterglow-sage.vercel.app/ , the "Getting to the Spots" section. The
  section offers two ways of finding a spot: the "Find nearest spot to me" button in the
  top right corner (browser geolocation), and the filter box below it.

- What I did, what I saw: Laptop Firefox, Sunrise mode, location already granted.
  I wanted to know which shooting spot was nearest to where I was at the time (near
  Somerset). I typed the road name of where I was, "Devonshire", into the filter box and
  got:
      No shooting spots found matching "Devonshire" facing east for sunrise.
  Other than granting the browser location permission, the page offers no way at all to
  enter where I am. The same behaviour reproduced in laptop Safari.

- Which heuristic: 9, Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors

- Screen or system: System. To work out how far Devonshire is from each shooting spot,
  the system first has to turn that piece of text into coordinates, and the page has
  never been sent anything of the kind.

- Severity, and why: 4, driven by whether the person can learn around it. There is no way
  on the page to get past granting location and still judge which spot is nearest, so a
  visitor who will not share their location can never answer that question.

- The repair: After a visitor enters where they are, the screen shows how far each
  shooting spot is from there and marks the nearest one.

### Finding C
- Where: https://afterglow-sage.vercel.app/ , the filter box at the top of the "Getting to
  the Spots" section. On a phone (iPhone).

- What I did, what I saw: I walked it once in the WeChat in-app browser and then once in
  Safari; the behaviour was the same in both.
  I tapped the filter box to type, and the page immediately zoomed in, pushing the content
  on the right off the screen. After I finished typing and dismissed the keyboard, the
  page stayed zoomed in. It never returns to its own scale, and every time I had to pinch
  it back myself.
  While zoomed in there were two concrete consequences:
    1. The empty-result sentence was cut off. I could only see
       "No shooting spots found matching "blk333"" and "west for sunset.", with the middle
       of the sentence out of view.
    2. The Clear button on the right of the input was pushed off the screen, so it could
       only be reached by first swiping sideways.
  I measured the font size of this input in the laptop Firefox console:
      getComputedStyle(document.querySelector('input[placeholder^="Filter spots"]')).fontSize
      → "14px"

- Which heuristic: 4, Consistency and Standards

- Screen or system: Screen. The font size of this input is the page's own styling, so the
  page already has everything it needs to put this right, with no new data.

- Severity, and why: 3, driven by whether the person can learn around it. Everyone who
  uses this box on a phone meets it, and they have to pinch the page back every single
  time; it cannot be learned around.

- The repair: After a visitor taps this box on a phone and finishes typing, the page
  returns to its own scale, and both the Clear button and the result message can be seen
  without swiping sideways.

## 3. My predictions

1. The three findings I expect my groupmates to raise, and the severity I expect them to
   give each:

   - Finding A — I expect them to rate it 3, because everyone who opens this section sees
     the words "or road" in the placeholder. A reviewer is likely to type a road name into
     the box and search first, and only afterwards tap the location button in the top right
     corner.

   - Finding B — I expect them to rate it 4, because most people are not willing to grant
     location the first time they are asked. A reviewer who declines will fall back to the
     filter box, and at that point there is no way on the page to tell which shooting spot
     is nearest to them.

   - Finding C — I expect them to rate it 3, because everyone who uses this box on a phone
     meets it. A reviewer who is somewhere unfamiliar can only search by what the street
     signs say, and has to pinch the page back every single time.

2. The heuristic I think my product breaks worst:

   9, Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors. Finding B is the one that
   sits under it, and it is the one I would fix first. The gap is on the system side: the
   page is never sent the coordinates it would need to answer where a visitor is. If that
   were closed, Finding A would stop happening too, because typing a road name would return
   a real answer instead of an empty result.

3. The finding that would show my own evaluation was wrong:

   If a groupmate reports that a Sky Score gives them no way to tell whether the number is
   high or low, under heuristic 6, Recognition Rather than Recall, at severity 3 or higher,
   my evaluation was wrong. Every finding I logged sits in the "Getting to the Spots"
   section, and I logged nothing at all against Tonight Forecast, even though that is where
   the product's central claim lives. I can read a score of 40 only because I have seen one
   of 89 on another evening. A visitor sees one number on one visit, with nothing to compare
   it against, and the page never puts that comparison on screen.
